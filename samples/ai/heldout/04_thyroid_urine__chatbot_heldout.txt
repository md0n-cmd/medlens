## 1\. Thyroid profile – The main concern

| Test | Your result | Reference range | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Total T3 | 1.10 | 0.80–2.00 | Normal |

### What does this mean?

Two results stand out:

- **TSH \<0.01:** Your TSH is significantly suppressed.
- **Free T4 = 2.1:** Your free thyroid hormone is higher than the normal range.

This combination is suggestive of **overt hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis)**.

In simple language, your thyroid gland may be producing more thyroid hormone than your body needs.

### What symptoms can it cause?

You might experience:

- Increased heartbeat or palpitations.
- Anxiety, nervousness or restlessness.
- Excessive sweating.
- Feeling unusually hot.
- Weight loss despite normal eating.
- Hand tremors.
- Difficulty sleeping.
- Frequent bowel movements.
- Irregular menstrual periods.

You may not experience all these symptoms.

### What could be causing it?

Some possible causes include:

1. **Graves' disease:** An autoimmune condition that makes the thyroid overactive.
2. **Thyroid inflammation (thyroiditis):** Can temporarily release excess thyroid hormones.
3. **Thyroid nodules:** Certain nodules can produce excessive hormones.
4. **Excess thyroid medication:** If someone is taking thyroid hormone tablets.

A doctor needs to investigate the underlying cause rather than assuming it is Graves' disease.

### What should you do?

I recommend seeing an endocrinologist or physician soon, ideally within the next few days.

They may recommend:

- Repeat TSH and Free T4.
- Free T3.
- Anti-TPO antibodies.
- TRAb (thyroid receptor antibodies) to investigate Graves' disease.
- Thyroid ultrasound if clinically indicated.

Don't start thyroid medication or supplements without medical advice.

If you experience chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness or a very rapid heartbeat, seek urgent medical care.

## 2\. Vitamin D – Lower than ideal

Your result:

- Vitamin D: **18 ng/mL**

Generally:

- Below 20: Low.
- 20–29: Insufficient by some clinical guidelines.
- 30 or above: Adequate for most people.

Your level is low and worth addressing.

Vitamin D deficiency can be associated with:

- Muscle aches.
- Bone discomfort.
- Muscle weakness.
- Fatigue.

Your doctor may recommend vitamin D supplementation depending on your circumstances. Avoid taking high doses on your own.
