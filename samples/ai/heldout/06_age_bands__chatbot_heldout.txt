### What does this mean?

Your TSH is slightly above the laboratory reference range.

However, your Free T4 and T3 are normal.

This pattern can suggest **subclinical hypothyroidism**.

Subclinical hypothyroidism

In simple language:

- Your thyroid hormone levels are currently within the normal range.
- Your brain is producing slightly more TSH to stimulate the thyroid gland.
- Your thyroid may be starting to function less efficiently.

### What symptoms might occur?

Some people experience:

- Tiredness.
- Weight gain.
- Hair fall.
- Dry skin.
- Constipation.
- Feeling unusually cold.
- Difficulty concentrating.
- Menstrual irregularities (in women).

However, many people with mildly elevated TSH have no symptoms.

### Is medication necessary?

Not necessarily.

A TSH of 5.2 does not automatically mean you need thyroid medication.

Your doctor may recommend:

1. Repeating TSH and Free T4 after approximately 2–3 months.
2. Testing Anti-TPO antibodies to check for autoimmune thyroid disease.
3. Reviewing symptoms and medical history.

Treatment decisions depend on factors such as symptoms, repeat TSH levels, antibodies and pregnancy status.

### What does this mean?

**HDL (good cholesterol): 45**

HDL helps transport cholesterol away from arteries.

- For men, above 40 is generally acceptable.
- For women, above 50 is generally preferred.

**Total cholesterol: 195**

Your total cholesterol is within the standard desirable range of below 200 mg/dL.

However, total cholesterol alone cannot determine cardiovascular risk.

It would be useful to check:

- LDL cholesterol.
- Triglycerides.
- Non-HDL cholesterol.

These provide a more complete picture.
